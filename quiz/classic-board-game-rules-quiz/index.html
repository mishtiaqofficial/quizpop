About this quiz

Gathering around the table for family game night is a cherished tradition, but chances are high that your family has been playing some of the most famous games wrong for decades. Many of the rules we take for granted in classics like Monopoly, Uno, and Scrabble are actually popular house rules passed down by word of mouth rather than the official rulebook. Putting cash on Free Parking in Monopoly? Stacking Draw Two cards in Uno? Most players believe these are standard moves, but official game makers have repeatedly set the record straight: they are not part of the original rules! House rules can be fun, but they often make games drag on much longer than intended. Are you ready to see how well you truly know the games in your closet? What are the most commonly played classic board game rules that are actually wrong? The most common rule mistake is putting tax money on Free Parking in Monopoly, which official rules say does nothing. Another huge misconception is stacking Draw Two cards in Uno, which the makers confirmed is an illegal move that was invented as a popular house rule.

Why do people play with house rules instead of official board game rules? Most people learn board games from friends or parents rather than reading the instruction booklet directly. Over decades, customized tweaks spread by word of mouth and became accepted as real rules, even though they often accidentally make games drag on much longer than the designers intended.

Does the Monopoly auction rule speed up gameplay? Yes, following the official auction rule speeds up Monopoly significantly. When an unowned property is auctioned immediately after a player passes on buying it, properties are purchased much faster. This builds color monopolies quickly, leading to higher rents and a quicker conclusion to the game.
